konomi-judge

Which criteria can a model grade for you — and which must a person keep?

"Can we replace human graders with an LLM judge" is asked, and answered, globally. That question has no useful answer: a judge checks JSON validity perfectly and cannot reliably tell you whether a response overclaimed its own certainty. The decision belongs to each criterion separately, and this makes it there.

Raw agreement lies

On a criterion met 95% of the time, a judge that answers MET unconditionally scores 94% agreement. It has learned nothing. Kappa corrects for exactly that, and scores it zero.

Direction is a separate failure

A judge can disagree symmetrically (noise) or systematically one way (bias). Bias shifts every score it touches. They need different fixes, so they are reported apart.

Too small is not a pass

A criterion scored on four items where the judge happened to agree reads as automatable. It is reported as undecided — which is how a rubric stops quietly being checked by anyone.

A judge that has learned nothing at all

Why both agreement figures

Automation requires raw agreement and Cohen's kappa to clear their thresholds, because either alone is gameable — raw agreement by an imbalanced criterion, kappa by a tiny sample. Kappa alone is also unintuitive, and both together are honest. A degenerate kappa — where both raters gave one verdict throughout — never automates: a criterion that has never once discriminated has not been tested at all.

Honest limits

  • This measures agreement with your reference, not correctness. If your human graders are wrong in a consistent way, a judge that matches them scores beautifully.
  • Kappa assumes the two raters are independent. A judge prompted with your grader's own guidelines is not fully independent of them.
  • Items where either side abstained are excluded and counted separately. Scoring an abstention as disagreement punishes a judge for correctly declining; scoring it as agreement inflates everything.
  • The thresholds are defaults, not law. Every one is overridable, and every decision names the figure that produced it so it can be argued with.
  • A saving is always reported with its residual error rate. A saving quoted without one is a sales figure, not an engineering one.

Pure kernels, no dependencies, no network. Nothing here calls a model — verdicts arrive as data, which is what lets the same code compare human to human.
